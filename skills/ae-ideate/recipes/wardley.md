# Wardley

Decide where custom work is worth the effort and where an existing solution is sufficient.

1. Start with the user and the need established in the brief. Trace the few capabilities needed to meet it and what each depends on. Include suppliers or data access when they constrain the idea.
2. Position capabilities by visibility to the user and how established they are in the market: experimental (**genesis**), bespoke (**custom-built**), repeatable offerings (**product**), or widely available and standardized (**commodity/utility**). Use evidence such as available suppliers, standard interfaces, substitutability, and customer expectations. Mark uncertain positions. A capability can be new to this team and routine in the market.
3. Use the competitor and substitute research to ask where existing offerings already satisfy the need, where users still struggle, and what the proposed product could do meaningfully better. Notice dependencies a supplier could restrict, increase in price, or absorb into its own product. Treat future market moves as hypotheses, not predictions.
4. Turn the map into a decision: what to experiment with, build, buy, reuse, or avoid; which dependency to check first; and how that changes the recommended idea. A routine component may still require custom work for a demonstrated constraint; explain the cost and reason.

Keep the map limited to components that affect the choice. When a map is requested, show dependency links, visibility vertically, and evolution horizontally. Otherwise include only the conclusions that change the Opportunity Brief. Competitor feature comparisons supply evidence; they do not replace the dependency map.
